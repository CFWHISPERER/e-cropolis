# E-Cropolis — Research Bibliography

**Established:** 2026-10-20  
**Status:** Living reference inventory; not a claim that every source has been fully reviewed.

## Purpose and conventions

This bibliography records works informing the E-Cropolis story, ecological research, and simulation design. It is a research guide, not evidence that a practice is suitable for the fictional 650-acre property near Monroe, Oregon.

For each entry, distinguish **published work** from **proposed E-Cropolis applications**. Local suitability, historical claims, material performance, and ecological outcomes require their own evidence. Add publication identifiers, editions, page references, and dates accessed when verified. Do not silently promote a candidate source into a validated finding.

## Books and design frameworks

### David Holmgren — *RetroSuburbia: The Downshifter's Guide to a Resilient Future* (2018)
- **Relevance:** Retrofitting existing settlements; household-scale food, energy, shelter, resource use, and community resilience.
- **E-Cropolis inquiry:** How might an ecovillage represent adaptive reuse, distributed capabilities, and material sufficiency rather than assuming every solution requires new infrastructure?
- **Status:** Foundational design reference; specific claims and page references not yet extracted.
- **Publisher/project:** Melliodora Publishing / [RetroSuburbia](https://retrosuburbia.com/).

### Bill Mollison — *Permaculture: A Designer's Manual* (1988)
- **Relevance:** Systems-oriented ecological design, site observation, water, climate, soils, productive landscapes, and integrated settlement planning.
- **E-Cropolis inquiry:** Which design principles can be translated into explicit, testable rules, and which must remain qualitative guidance?
- **Status:** Foundational design reference; no endorsement of untested modeling assumptions.

### David Fleming — *Lean Logic: A Dictionary for the Future and How to Survive It* (2016, posthumous publication; edited by Shaun Chamberlin)
- **Relevance:** Culture, economics, energy, resilience, community, and ways of living beyond narrow measures of monetary output.
- **E-Cropolis inquiry:** How might a simulation make room for informal cooperation, capability, ecological continuity, and meaningful participation?
- **Status:** Conceptual reference; normative arguments are not empirical measurements.

## Willow biomass, restoration, and renewable materials

### SUNY College of Environmental Science and Forestry — Willow Biomass Project
- **Research program:** Short-rotation shrub willow production, breeding, crop management, renewable products, and environmental performance.
- **Source:** [SUNY-ESF Willow Project](https://www.esf.edu/willow/).
- **E-Cropolis inquiry:** Could dedicated, appropriately sited willow coppice provide renewable fencing, woven material, poles, or other biomass while existing riparian willow communities remain protected?
- **Important distinction:** Short-rotation biomass research does not by itself establish structural-construction suitability, local yield, or ecological benefit in Oregon.
- **Status:** Candidate research program; specific locally transferable findings require review.

### Volk, T. A., Heavey, J. P., and Eisenbies, M. H. — “Advances in shrub-willow crops for bioenergy, renewable products, and environmental benefits” (2016)
- **Publication:** *Food and Energy Security*.
- **Relevance:** Review of shrub willow production systems, management, products, and environmental questions.
- **E-Cropolis inquiry:** What parts of managed coppice research can inform a monitored willow-material system, and which depend on local soils, water supply, species, and management?
- **Status:** Identified scholarly reference; findings have not been independently evaluated for the Monroe study area.

## Environmental evidence sources — candidate datasets

## Historical and regional research leads

### Oregon hops and Willamette Valley agriculture
- **Research question:** What evidence supports nineteenth-century hop cultivation and trade in the southern Willamette Valley, particularly near Monroe, and transatlantic links to British brewing?
- **Candidate secondary source:** Peter A. Kopp, *Hoptopia: A World of Agriculture and Beer in Oregon's Willamette Valley* (2016).
- **Fiction/evidence boundary:** The Bancroft family, its landholding, and an ancestor's hop-growing ambitions are fictional. Do not infer purchases by any specific Lancashire brewery without primary evidence.
- **Status:** Historical lead; local land-use and trading claims not yet verified.

## Open research questions

1. Which official watershed/subwatershed boundary best represents the Long Tom–Willamette confluence near Monroe?
2. Which riparian willow species are documented locally, and where might cultivation avoid sensitive habitats?
3. What evidence is needed to estimate net annual or rotational harvestable willow material after accounting for water, soil, biodiversity, and maintenance?
4. How can regenerative capability be represented without conflating model output, historical inference, and observed ecological evidence?

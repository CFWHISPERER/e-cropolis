# Integrated Food–Water–Energy Systems

**Status:** Research reference; exploratory only  
**Project:** Project Lichen / E-Cropolis  
**Added:** 2026-10-24  
**Implementation commitment:** None

## Purpose

Collect evidence about infrastructure that supports multiple functions—food production, water stewardship, and energy generation—without assuming that multifunctionality alone guarantees ecological regeneration.

## Research reference: aquavoltaics

- **Source:** *Nature Food*, article DOI [10.1038/s43016-026-01446-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43016-026-01446-6)
- **Topic:** Aquavoltaics: integrating photovoltaic electricity generation with freshwater aquaculture.
- **Evidence status:** Research lead for further examination. Verify quantitative estimates, assumptions, methods, and supplementary data against the full paper before reusing figures in modeling or public claims.

### Relevance

Solar panels installed over managed aquaculture ponds may allow electricity production and food production to share an existing footprint. Shading may also affect evaporation, water temperature, dissolved oxygen, algae, aquatic biodiversity, and productivity. These effects are context-dependent and require site-specific evaluation.

This is relevant to Project Lichen's interest in integrated living and energy systems, distributed infrastructure, and recursive regenerative capability. For E-Cropolis, it suggests a *possible* future simulation of managed water infrastructure coupled to a local microgrid.

### Questions to investigate

1. What are the paper's geographical, area-coverage, irradiance, and electricity-conversion assumptions?
2. How do seasonal shading and temperature changes affect aquatic food production, oxygen, and ecosystem health?
3. What are the embodied-material, maintenance, replacement, and end-of-life costs of the installation?
4. How much generated electricity can be used locally, and what storage or grid integration is required?
5. How should the model distinguish avoided grid emissions from actual ecological improvement?
6. Could the arrangement preserve or enlarge long-term ecological and productive capabilities rather than merely increase short-term output?

## Possible future E-Cropolis experiment

**Working title:** Multifunctional Water Infrastructure  
**Status:** Candidate experiment; not scheduled or approved for implementation.

**Hypothesis to test:** Under some site-specific conditions, photovoltaic installations over *existing managed artificial ponds* could provide useful local electricity while maintaining or improving selected water-management and food-production outcomes compared with separately sited infrastructure.

**Proposed comparisons:** baseline pond; pond with partial PV coverage; separate ground-mounted PV plus pond. Compare energy yield, land occupation, water conditions, food productivity, materials, maintenance, and ecological indicators. Report trade-offs and uncertainty rather than combining unlike outcomes into one unqualified score.

**Site integrity constraint:** The fictional 650-acre E-Cropolis property near Monroe, Oregon, is not evidence of any actual pond, ownership boundary, or suitable installation site. Do not assume suitability without geospatial and ecological investigation. Natural wetlands, streams, and other sensitive habitats are not interchangeable with managed aquaculture ponds and should not be treated as available solar-development surfaces.

## Epistemic integrity

- Distinguish modeled technical potential from observed field outcomes.
- Distinguish greenhouse-gas offsets from net ecological regeneration.
- Record assumptions, data provenance, scale, and uncertainty.
- Keep fictional E-Cropolis scenarios separate from empirical Monroe-area evidence.
- Do not add simulation requirements until a separate experiment proposal is reviewed.

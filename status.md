
# E-Cropolis — Project Status

**Project:** E-Cropolis  
**Part of:** Project Lichen  
**Last updated:** October 9, 2026  
**Current phase:** Foundation and discovery  
**Development status:** Pre-prototype

> **Guiding question:** What might people build here if they began by asking what was already here?

## 1. Project Overview

E-Cropolis is an open exploration of human settlement, ecological regeneration, distributed intelligence and governance through storytelling, scientific inquiry and computer simulation.

The project seeks to investigate how human communities might develop while maintaining or enlarging the capabilities of the living systems upon which they depend.

E-Cropolis is not intended to demonstrate that any particular philosophy of settlement is inherently superior.

Instead, it aims to provide an evidence-grounded environment in which different approaches can be explored, compared, questioned and revised.

The project combines three complementary activities:

- **Narrative:** A developing story introducing the people, landscape and questions behind E-Cropolis.
- **Research:** Investigation of real ecological, agricultural, technological and social systems.
- **Simulation:** Development of an interactive environment in which decisions and their consequences can be explored.

## 3. Foundational Principle: Epistemic Integrity

E-Cropolis must distinguish between:

1. Observable facts and measurements.
2. Scientific models and their assumptions.
3. Estimates and predictions.
4. Fictional narrative elements.
5. Hypotheses awaiting investigation.

Models should expose their assumptions and limitations wherever practical.

Results must not be presented as validated simply because they support the project's preferred ideas.

**A failed hypothesis is a successful contribution to understanding when its failure is properly documented.**

## 4. Geographical Foundation

The initial simulation landscape will be inspired by the southern Willamette Valley near Monroe, Oregon.

The narrative introduces a fictional 650-acre property inherited by Bert Bancroft.

The landscape may include:

- A tributary connected to the Willamette River watershed.
- Agricultural terraces and cultivated land.
- Riparian ecosystems.
- Seasonal wetlands.
- Woodland and wildlife habitat.
- Areas potentially suitable for settlement.

These features remain **proposed characteristics**, subject to geographical and ecological research.

The property's ownership and boundaries are fictional. Real-world data will inform environmental modeling, and public-land boundaries will be considered as a research input.

No actual private property is represented as available for development.

## 5. Simulation Development

### Candidate foundation: MicropolisCore

E-Cropolis is investigating MicropolisCore, an open-source descendant of the original SimCity simulation.

Potential development areas include:

| System | Research direction |
|---|---|
| Land | Soil characteristics, land use and ecological capability |
| Water | Watersheds, precipitation, infiltration and seasonal flows |
| Food | Regional foodsheds, agricultural production and distribution |
| Energy | Generation, storage, distribution and resource consumption |
| Ecology | Habitat, biodiversity and ecosystem relationships |
| Communities | Population, needs, cooperation and social organization |
| Governance | Distributed decision-making and scales of consequence |
| Information | Data Distance, epistemic integrity and local intelligence |

These are research directions, not implemented features.

**Current technical milestone:** Establish a reproducible baseline of the unmodified MicropolisCore simulation before introducing E-Cropolis-specific changes.

## 6. Research Questions

Our initial investigations include:

**Ecological capability:** How can a settlement maintain or enlarge the regenerative capabilities of its surrounding landscape?

**Agriculture:** What happens when agricultural decisions account for soil, biodiversity, water and long-term ecological functioning alongside food production?

**Data Distance:** How does the location and movement of information affect resource consumption, decision quality and responsiveness?

**Governance:** At what scale should decisions be made when their consequences extend beyond individual households or communities?

**Simulation integrity:** How can assumptions, uncertainties and evidence be made visible to participants?

These questions remain open.

## 8. Decisions and Open Questions

### Decisions recorded

**October 9, 2026 — Geographical approach**

Retain Bert's fictional 650-acre property near Monroe, Oregon.

Use real watershed, soil and ecological data to inform the simulation.

Consider public-land boundaries as a research input rather than requiring the fictional property to occupy actual public land.

**October 9, 2026 — Public project documentation**

Use a dedicated GitHub repository for development records and retain the existing Project Lichen E-Cropolis page as the public-facing project introduction.

### Open questions

- Which specific watershed provides the most suitable ecological reference?
- Which MicropolisCore components can be reused without substantial modification?
- How should ecological capability be represented and measured?
- How can simulation results be compared with real-world observations?
- What should constitute the first meaningful E-Cropolis experiment?

## 9. How Progress Will Be Reported

Each substantial update should record:

**What changed?**  
The work completed or decision made.

**What did we learn?**  
New findings, including unexpected outcomes.

**What supports the finding?**  
Evidence, data, references or reproducible experiments.

**What remains uncertain?**  
Known limitations, unresolved questions and competing explanations.

**What comes next?**  
The next investigation or development milestone.
